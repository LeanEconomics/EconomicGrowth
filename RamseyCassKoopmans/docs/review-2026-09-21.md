# Second review: Ramsey–Cass–Koopmans

The September 21, 2026 review traced the general Cass construction from
primitive assumptions through the clipped ODE, removal of clipping, finite
welfare, optimality, uniqueness, and convergence. It also reviewed the
stationary and explicit-economy results, the distinction between finite-welfare
optimality and all-competitor asymptotic comparison, and the Koopmans boundary
obstruction. No mathematical defect was found in those reviewed proof chains.

Visual source checks covered Cass pp. 233–234 and 237–238, and Koopmans
pp. 246, 248, 250, 261–263, and 276–279. The source map now makes the formal
theorem's extra explicit regularity assumptions easier to compare with Cass:
`f(0)=0`, continuity of the relevant derivatives, and a regular competitor
class with positive consumption and continuous controls. Cass's displayed
feasible class permits zero consumption. The formal result does not claim
coverage of every admissible control in the original papers.

The Koopmans terminal-sign discrepancy and the assumptions used by the
boundary obstruction match the inspected pages. The obstruction concerns
regular convergent Euler paths under the displayed assumptions; it is not a
proof that every alternative formulation of the optimization problem fails.
The source map also corrects module names previously used as namespaces.

The old declaration scanner omitted four structure types. The revised audit
covers **423 theorems and 576 declarations**, including the types,
constructors, and field projections. All module and root-import inventories
are checked, and failure checks remain active under Python optimization.
Only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` are permitted.

The 24 TheoryDebugger diagnostics still provide 48 certificates. The normal
audit now freshly compiles those sources, checks their proof types against the
original JSON inputs, and audits their actual dependencies. Stored success
labels and source hashes alone cannot pass. Regression tests include a real
Lean rejection of an unrelated proof substituted into otherwise successful
saved evidence. The source hashes and current successful audit are recorded
in `verification/verification.json`, and CI repeats the tests and audit.

Mathematical statements and proof bodies were unchanged by this review.
The stronger verification infrastructure improves reproducibility; assessing
economic assumptions and source fidelity remains part of researcher review.

## September 22: LeanEconomics conventions follow-up

A separate comparison with the main LeanEconomics project's
[working conventions](https://github.com/LeanEconomics/LeanEconomics/blob/main/CLAUDE.md)
found that this project's build did not enable the standard Mathlib lint set.
Running that set exposed sixteen long lines and one tactic needing explicit
goal focusing. Those issues are corrected, and the numerical helper `R` is now
named `inverseFactor`, with its associated lemmas and references renamed.
The economic assumptions, conclusions, and helper formulas are unchanged.

The library build and fresh-source audit now both enable the standard lint set.
The audit passes the options explicitly to Lean and records them in
`verification/verification.json`. Only the Apache-specific header rule is
disabled, consistently with the other growth projects and this contribution's
Unlicense. Generated arithmetic certificates retain their separate kernel and
axiom checks; they are not included in the handwritten source's style claim.

This aligns the checked coding conventions while retaining the independent
Lean 4.34.0 project. It does not assert compatibility with the main library's
different pinned toolchain or substitute for maintainer review.
